Abstract: To address the vulnerability of physical unclonable functions (PUFs) to reverse localization attacks arising from their regular circuit and layout features, this paper proposes a feature-eliminated key-concealment method based on a weak Arbiter PUF. The proposed method takes advantage of the commonly used and non-distinctive MUX- and DFF-based structure of the Arbiter PUF, making it well suited for integration into digital circuits. By combining circuit reuse and randomized layout, the key PUF units are dispersed and concealed within large-scale digital circuits, thereby eliminating both the circuit-level and image-level features of the PUF. To suppress the systematic path bias introduced by circuit reuse and randomized placement, a path-hedging compensation technique is further proposed, which compresses the residual delay mismatch to the 10 ps level and thus preserves the uniformity and uniqueness of the output responses. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method enables the concealment of weak Arbiter PUF units in digital circuits. The concealed PUF exhibits a Bias of 0.4691, an inter-chip Hamming distance (HD) of 0.4970, an intra-chip HD of 0.0821, and a BER of 0.0838.

Keywords: Key concealment; Layout randomization; Physical unclonable function (PUF); Arbiter PUF; Feature elimination
